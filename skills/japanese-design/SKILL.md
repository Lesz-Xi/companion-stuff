# Japanese Design

Current name: `japanese-design`.

This skill previously carried a full Hara/Relics doctrinal framework (ex-formation method,
Relics living-specimen language, motion numbers, external-reference gate). That apparatus and
its reference files have been retired at Chief's direction — broader inspiration now lives in
`Developer/Ulysses`, not one anchored doctrine. Two pieces of that framework earned their keep
and are retained below.

## Hierarchy

```
think-different (representation)
  → product-design (function)
    → japanese-design (surface)   ← this skill
      → web-design (craft)
twin-sparrow-taste (identity) cuts across
```

This skill decides **how it feels** at the surface layer: spacing and hover. `web-design`
implements numbers and wiring and never overrides surface. `product-design` owns value order
and the primary action.

## Retained Principle 1 — Minimal-spacing hierarchy

Space is active, not leftover. Prefer hairlines and tone over boxes, elevation, and drop
shadows. Give the most important elements generous room rather than filling gaps because they
exist. This governs vertical rhythm and plane separation at the surface layer, under the
hierarchy above — it does not license decoration.

## Retained Principle 2 — Hover button effect

Hover reads as quiet attention, not spectacle:

- Ink/opacity shift and a hairline reveal on quiet hover — not rounded pills or dashboard tabs.
- Primary buttons: fill rises from below (bottom-up reveal), no glow.
- Hover never invents a second primary action.

## Non-Goals

Do not invoke for pure token nits, copy fixes, or engineering bugs with no surface judgment.
Execute those directly. Do not treat this skill as a full design-doctrine source — it covers
spacing and hover only; broader taste work should draw from `Developer/Ulysses` instead.

## Output Expectations

- Name which of the two retained principles applied (spacing, hover, or both)
- Actionable; compact
- Do not reintroduce Hara/Relics-derived vocabulary (ma, shibui, seijaku, ex-formation, etc.)
  as if it were still this skill's grounding — it isn't, beyond the two principles above
